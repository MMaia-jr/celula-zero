## Purpose

Make the path after `HABITABLE-ALPHA-001` explicit without turning future
possibilities into promised features, dates, adoption claims or scale claims.

The central coordination question is:

> **Which evidence gate does this work unlock?**

A work item that cannot answer that question should normally be parked unless it
preserves a concrete property, closes a current risk or supports a separately
authorized experiment.

This roadmap is an evidence map, not a feature calendar.

## Gate-status discipline

For each gate preserve, when applicable:

`NOT TESTED → PREPARED → EXECUTED → VERIFIED → PASS / FAIL / PARTIAL / INCONCLUSIVE`

Do not infer a later gate from an earlier one.

Also preserve the broader evidence ladder:

`DOCUMENTED → REPRESENTED → EXECUTED → VERIFIED → REPLICATED → USEFUL TO EXTERNAL USER → ADOPTED → SCALABLE`

A gate may provide evidence relevant to a later rung without automatically
promoting the project to that rung.

## G1 — External entry + voluntary action

State:

`CURRENT / NOT YET PASSED`

Question:

> Does a real external person actually enter the relevant context and
> voluntarily take a real action?

Required observations:

1. `ENTRY` observed separately from outreach;
2. `ACTION` observed separately from interest.

Important boundaries:

- outreach published ≠ entry;
- message sent ≠ participant action;
- view/click/like/signup ≠ real-world consequence;
- no synthetic participant or fabricated opportunity.

Current candidate context:

`ResoVerse Commons #4`, subject to actual external response/behavior.

Exit condition:

`ENTRY` and voluntary `ACTION` are both observed and attributable to a real
external participant.

If entry or action does not occur, record the result. Do not build a larger
product merely to convert silence into activity.

A useful one-shot result does **not** yet establish full habitability because
`RETURN` may still be unobserved.

## G4 — Replication

State:

`NOT YET TESTED`

Question:

> Does the useful/habitable behavior survive a deliberately different real
> participant or context?

Minimum logic:

- use at least one additional real context materially different from the first;
- preserve the core claim being tested;
- document adaptations instead of hiding them;
- reproduce external utility/habitability without treating the first case as a
  universal template.

Exit condition:

A second bounded real context reproduces the relevant result strongly enough to
classify the behavior as replicated under stated limits.

Failure here means the earlier result may be context-specific. It does not erase
the earlier result.

`REPLICATED ≠ ADOPTED`.

## G5 — Recurring participation

State:

`NOT YET TESTED`

Question:

> Do external participants voluntarily re-engage across more than one useful
> cycle because the context remains valuable?

Required distinction:

- reminder-driven return ≠ spontaneous recurring utility;
- one return event ≠ recurring participation;
- founder orchestration ≠ self-sustaining participation.

Before executing this gate, preregister a concrete recurrence threshold
appropriate to the observed context rather than inventing a number now.

Exit condition:

Repeated real participation is observed across time with a reason to return that
is tied to real utility rather than artificial incentives or telemetry.

## G6 — First habitable community

State:

`NOT YET TESTED`

Question:

> Can a small group coordinate around real needs, projects and contributions
> without the founder being the sole bridge for every useful relation?

Evidence should include:

- more than one external participant;
- participant-to-participant relation or coordination;
- real work/need/opportunity rather than simulated activity;
- contextual consent and authority;
- observable contribution/result/history;
- recurring participation;
- a legitimate path for a newcomer to enter.

Preserve:

- projects belong to their creators absent explicit agreement;
- sponsorship ≠ endorsement ≠ contribution ≠ economic right;
- community ≠ DAO;
- community ≠ universal governance system.

Exit condition:

A bounded group repeatedly produces useful real coordination and can admit or
orient another participant without requiring the founder to manually mediate
every meaningful interaction.

## G7 — Productization

State:

`NOT YET TESTED`

Question:

> Which repeated properties demonstrated by real use deserve a stable product
> surface?

Only productize capabilities that survive prior gates or preserve a concrete
observed property loss.

For each candidate capability classify:

`ADOPT / MAP / EXTEND / MISSING`

Prefer:

`observed need → smallest stable capability → test → result`

Possible exit evidence:

- stable user-facing path for the repeated core cycle;
- reproducible operation;
- privacy/security/data boundaries appropriate to actual use;
- support and recovery expectations defined;
- external users can complete the relevant flow without founder-only technical
  intervention.

This gate does not require every historical feature idea.

`PRODUCTIZED ≠ PMF`.

## G8 — Independent acquisition

State:

`NOT YET TESTED`

Question:

> Can a new external participant enter through a public or participant-mediated
> path without direct one-to-one founder recruitment and still receive utility?

Possible channels may include:

- public repository/project entry;
- participant referral;
- public project/opportunity;
- external community or partner context.

Channel choice is an experiment, not architecture.

Exit condition:

At least one real external participant independently enters, acts and reaches
the relevant utility criterion without being personally recruited and walked
through the full experience by the founder.

`INDEPENDENT ENTRY ≠ ADOPTION`.

## G9 — Adoption

State:

`NOT YET TESTED`

Question:

> Is useful recurring participation happening across enough independent real
> people/contexts to justify an adoption claim?

Before testing adoption, preregister quantitative and qualitative thresholds
based on the usage pattern actually discovered. Do not retroactively choose a
threshold that makes existing activity look like adoption.

An adoption claim should require evidence combining:

- external utility;
- recurrence;
- replication across distinct participants/contexts;
- some independent acquisition;
- continued use not reducible to founder orchestration.

Exit classification must preserve scope, population, period and context.

`ADOPTED ≠ PMF ≠ GLOBAL ADOPTION ≠ SCALABLE`.

## G10 — Scale readiness

State:

`NOT YET TESTED`

Question:

> After adoption evidence exists, what concrete technical, social, legal,
> operational or economic properties block a larger experiment?

Do not build scale architecture in anticipation.

First measure actual bottlenecks such as:

- reliability and recovery;
- privacy/data governance;
- security and abuse;
- moderation/support;
- founder/operator bottlenecks;
- cost and operational load;
- interoperability/exportability;
- jurisdiction/context differences;
- preservation of provenance, authority and contextual trust under growth.

Exit condition:

Concrete bottlenecks and thresholds are measured well enough to authorize a
bounded scale experiment.

`SCALE READY ≠ SCALED`.

## Beyond G10 — global adoption is not yet an operational gate

Global adoption remains a possible long-term direction, not a status or promise.

After G10, region-, language-, jurisdiction- and community-specific expansion
experiments can be defined from evidence actually observed. A Brazilian or
single-community result must not be silently generalized to the world.

Do not preregister a fictional global rollout before the project knows which
properties actually need to survive expansion.

## Work-selection rule

Every material proposed work item should answer:

1. **Current gate** — which gate are we on?
2. **Uncertainty/property** — what exactly is unknown or being lost?
3. **Why this work now** — how does it unlock the gate?
4. **Simpler alternative** — can process/standard/existing infrastructure do it?
5. **Expected evidence** — what observable result will exist?
6. **Falsifier** — what would show the work was unnecessary or the hypothesis
   wrong?
7. **STOP gate** — when do we stop rather than expand scope?

If a work item unlocks no current gate and preserves no concrete property, park
it.

## Parallel work

Waiting on an external participant does not prohibit bounded parallel work.

Parallel work is legitimate when it:

- closes a real security/privacy risk;
- preserves recoverable history or current state;
- removes a demonstrated contributor/comprehension barrier;
- answers a specific uncertainty required by a current gate;
- belongs to a separately authorized experiment with explicit boundaries.

Parallel work must not fabricate external utility, reinterpret silence as
engagement or build future architecture merely because capacity is available.
